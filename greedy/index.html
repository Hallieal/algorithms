Further Reading

Four complementary intuition-first books, then deeper theory

Coding Interview Patterns is strongest on recognition and interview-ready invariants; Karumanchi is strongest as a counterexample-and-problem workbook; Grokking Algorithms emphasizes visual intuition and approximation; A Common-Sense Guide emphasizes deriving faster code from a slower baseline. The formal texts below supply deeper proof theory.

Narasimha Karumanchi — Data Structures and Algorithms Made EasyEspecially Chapter 17: a problem-driven greedy chapter with optimal file merging, interval scheduling and coloring, coin-change failures, fractional knapsack, railway platforms, road coverage, and scheduling examples; the graph chapter adds practical MST and Dijkstra comparisons.
Alex Xu & Shaun Gunawardane — Coding Interview PatternsEspecially Chapter 16 for Jump to the End, Gas Stations, and Candies; Chapter 9 for intervals and sweep-line reasoning; Chapter 15 for DP contrasts such as maximum subarray and 0/1 Knapsack; and the heap/Union-Find chapters for supporting data structures.
Jay Wengrow — A Common-Sense Guide to Data Structures and Algorithms in PythonEspecially Chapter 20: current Big O → best-imaginable Big O → pattern/state compression, plus Chapter 18 for a careful Dijkstra walkthrough and the array-vs-priority-queue implementation trade-off.
Aditya Y. Bhargava — Grokking Algorithms, 2nd ed.Especially Chapter 10 on greedy algorithms, Chapter 9 on Dijkstra, and the Huffman discussion in Chapter 7. Strong on visual intuition, “try it yourself” moments, and the transition from exact greedy to approximation.
Jon Kleinberg & Eva Tardos — Algorithm DesignEspecially Chapter 4: scheduling, exchange arguments, MSTs, and shortest paths. Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest & Stein — Introduction to AlgorithmsGreedy algorithms, Huffman coding, MSTs, and single-source shortest paths. RFC 1951 — DEFLATEA concrete real-world use of Huffman code lengths and deterministic code construction.

Chapter 9

Greedy Algorithms

Across 11 articles, this chapter treats greedy algorithms as a design method rather than a list of tricks: propose a local rule, attack it with counterexamples, identify the invariant, and decide whether the result is exact, approximate, or merely heuristic.

Further Reading

Start with intuition, then go deeper

For a visual and example-first companion, start with Grokking Algorithms. For more formal proofs and deeper theory, continue with the standard algorithm-design texts below.

Aditya Y. Bhargava — Grokking Algorithms, 2nd ed.Especially Chapter 10 on greedy algorithms, Chapter 9 on Dijkstra, and the Huffman discussion in Chapter 7. Strong on visual intuition, “try it yourself” moments, and the transition from exact greedy to approximation.
Jon Kleinberg & Eva Tardos — Algorithm DesignEspecially Chapter 4: scheduling, exchange arguments, MSTs, and shortest paths. Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest & Stein — Introduction to AlgorithmsGreedy algorithms, Huffman coding, MSTs, and single-source shortest paths. RFC 1951 — DEFLATEA concrete real-world use of Huffman code lengths and deterministic code construction.

Graph Models and Definitions

A graph records objects and the relationships between them. The objects are vertices; the relationships are edges.

Graph Models

Road maps, airline routes, program control flow, and social networks have the same abstract form: objects connected to other objects.

The modeling question is: what does a vertex represent, and what does an edge represent?

Definition

A graph G = (V, E) consists of a set V of vertices (also called nodes) and a set E of edges. In an undirected graph an edge joining u and v is the two-element set {u, v}. The two vertices are the ends of the edge.

Graph. G = (V, E), where V is the vertex set and E is a collection of pairs of vertices.

Two vertices are adjacent (or neighbors) if an edge connects them. An edge is incident to each of its endpoints. The degree of a vertex is the number of edges incident to it.

Example

V = {a, b, c, d}

E = {{a, b}, {b, c}, {c, d}, {d, a}, {a, c}}

The degree of a is 3. Vertices b and d are not adjacent.

Directed and undirected graphs

Edges in an undirected graph express a symmetric relationship. Sometimes the relationship is not symmetric. A one-way street, a hyperlink, or a prerequisite has a direction. In a directed graph, an edge is an ordered pair (u,v): it leaves u and enters v.

Undirected

If {u,v} is an edge, the relationship goes both ways. Typical examples are friendship, a cable, or a two-way road.

Directed

(u,v) and (v,u) are different edges. Typical examples are follows, hyperlinks, prerequisites, and one-way streets.

For a directed vertex, we distinguish its in-degree, the number of edges entering it, and its out-degree, the number of edges leaving it.

Weighted and unweighted graphs

Sometimes it is enough to know whether an edge exists. In other problems an edge carries a numerical value. This value is called its weight, and it can represent distance, travel time, price, capacity, or another quantity relevant to the problem.

Paths, cycles, and connectivity

A path is a sequence of vertices in which each consecutive pair is joined by an edge. A simple path has no repeated vertices. A cycle is a path that returns to its starting vertex; a simple cycle has no repeated vertices except for the first and last.

Two vertices are connected if there is a path between them. An undirected graph is connected if every vertex can be reached from every other vertex. If it is not connected, it breaks into connected components.

∑v∈V deg(v) = 2|E|

In an undirected graph every edge contributes one to the degree of each endpoint. Therefore the sum of all vertex degrees is exactly twice the number of edges.

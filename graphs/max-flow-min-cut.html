Max-Flow Min-Cut

A cut gives an upper bound on every feasible flow. The theorem says the best flow always meets the smallest such bound exactly.

Cuts

An s–t cut partitions the vertices into two sets S and T, with s∈S and t∈T.

Its capacity counts only original edges directed from the source side to the sink side:

c(S,T)=Σu∈S,v∈Tc(u,v)

Edges directed from T back into S are not added to the cut capacity.

Every cut bounds every flow

Every unit that reaches t must somehow cross from S to T. Therefore no feasible flow can exceed the capacity of any s–t cut.

|f| ≤ c(S,T)

Thus every s–t cut gives an upper bound on the value of a feasible flow.

The theorem

Max-flow min-cut theorem. The maximum value of an s–t flow equals the minimum capacity of an s–t cut.

max |f| = min c(S,T)

The theorem states that this upper bound is attained: some minimum cut has capacity equal to the maximum-flow value.

Proof from the residual graph

Run an augmenting-path algorithm until there is no residual path from s to t.

Let S be the set of vertices still reachable from s by positive-residual-capacity edges. Put every other vertex in T. Then t∈T.

Every original edge from S to T is saturated; otherwise that edge would let us reach another vertex of T.

Every original edge from T to S carries zero flow; otherwise its reverse residual edge would go from S to T.

Thus the net flow crossing the cut equals its capacity. Since no flow can exceed a cut, this flow and this cut are both optimal.

Recovering a minimum cut

After maximum flow is computed, perform one final BFS or DFS from s in the residual graph. Reached vertices form S; unreached vertices form T.

Optimality certificates

Primal certificate

A feasible flow of value F proves that value F is achievable.

Dual certificate

A cut of capacity F proves that no flow can have value larger than F.

When the two numbers match, no further argument is needed: both objects are optimal.

Flow and cut as matching bounds

A feasible flow gives a value that can be achieved, while a cut gives a value that cannot be exceeded. The theorem says that the best lower and upper bounds coincide.

The residual graph connects the two objects. During the algorithm it identifies possible augmentations; after the algorithm stops, reachability from s identifies a minimum cut.

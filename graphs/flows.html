Flow Networks and Residual Graphs

A flow network is a directed graph with limited-capacity edges. The central new idea is the residual graph: it records both unused capacity and the possibility of undoing earlier choices.

Flow networks

Start with a directed graph. Choose one vertex as the source s, one as the sink t, and assign a nonnegative capacity to every directed edge.

G=(V,E)the directed network
c(u,v)capacity of edge u→v
f(u,v)flow currently sent through it
|f|total amount sent from s to t

The number on an edge is not a distance. It is a limit. If c(u,v)=7, at most seven units may move from u to v.

For this chapter you only need directed graphs, paths, adjacency lists, and BFS/DFS. Dynamic programming is not a prerequisite.

Feasible flow

A collection of numbers on the edges is a valid flow only if two conditions hold everywhere in the network.

Capacity constraint

An edge cannot carry a negative amount and cannot exceed its capacity.

0 ≤ f(u,v) ≤ c(u,v)
Flow conservation

At every vertex other than s and t, total inflow equals total outflow.

Σu f(u,v)=Σw f(v,w)
The source and sink are the only exceptions: the source has net outflow, the sink has net inflow. Every other vertex is only a transit point.

Reading a flow

Once a flow is chosen, it is convenient to label each edge as f/c: current flow first, capacity second.

4 / 5
means: 4 units are used out of capacity 5

The value of a flow is the total amount leaving the source. By conservation, the same amount enters the sink.

|f|=Σvf(s,v)=Σuf(u,t)

The maximum-flow problem asks for a feasible flow with the largest possible value.

Residual networks

Suppose an edge has capacity 10 and currently carries 6 units. The original edge alone does not tell us everything the algorithm may still do. Two possibilities remain: send four more units forward, or cancel some of the six units already sent.

Residual graph

Forward residual capacity

How much additional flow can still be sent on the original edge.

cf(u,v)=c(u,v)-f(u,v)
Reverse residual capacity

How much of the current flow can be cancelled.

cf(v,u)=f(u,v)

Residual graph. Keep every direction whose residual capacity is positive. It describes every local change that can still be made to the current flow.

Why reverse edges matter

This is the part that makes flow algorithms different from simply finding several paths. An early path can be a bad choice, and the algorithm must be able to revise it.

A reverse residual edge does not mean that the original network contains a physical edge in that direction. It means: “we are allowed to undo this much of an earlier decision.”

Augmenting paths

An augmenting path is a path from s to t in the residual graph such that every edge on the path has positive residual capacity.

The whole path can be increased only as much as its smallest residual capacity:

Here Δ=4. Pushing four units along the path increases the total flow by four and changes the residual graph.

From the model to the algorithm

At this point the maximum-flow strategy is almost forced by the definitions.

→

Rebuild the residual possibilitiesForward capacity shrinks; reverse capacity grows.

Keep one picture in mind: the original graph says what is allowed in principle; the current flow says what we are doing now; the residual graph says what we are still allowed to change.

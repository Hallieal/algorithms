Minimum Spanning Trees

Graphs turn problems about connections, routes, and dependencies into standard algorithmic models.

Connect all vertices with minimum total cost

A spanning tree uses graph edges to connect every vertex without cycles. Every tree on V vertices has exactly V − 1 edges. An MST minimizes the sum of their weights.

Do not confuse MST with shortest paths. An MST minimizes the total cost of the whole connecting network. A shortest-path tree minimizes distances from one chosen source. These are different objectives.

Kruskal and Prim

Kruskal

Sort edges by weight. Add the next lightest edge unless it creates a cycle. Union-Find maintains the current components efficiently.

Prim

Grow one tree. Repeatedly add the cheapest edge crossing from the current tree to a new vertex. A priority queue makes the selection efficient.

The cut property

Split the vertices into two nonempty groups. Among the edges crossing that cut, a minimum-weight edge is safe: there exists an MST containing it. This is the key correctness principle behind both Kruskal and Prim.

Use MST when the objective is global connection cost: network design, wiring, roads, clustering, and building a cheap backbone.

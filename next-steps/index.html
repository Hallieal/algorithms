Where to Go Next

The core topics give you a strong working toolkit. From here, the best next step is not “more algorithms” in the abstract, but a sequence of families that build on ideas you already know.

A practical roadmap

01

Recursion & Divide and Conquer

Recurrences, recursion trees, Master-theorem intuition, and classic divide-and-conquer patterns beyond Merge Sort.

02

Trees & Search Trees

BSTs, balanced trees, ordered sets and maps, tree traversals, and why height controls operation costs.

03

Graph Algorithms

Shortest paths, topological order, strongly connected components, minimum spanning trees, and disjoint-set union.

04

Range-query Structures

Fenwick trees, segment trees, sparse tables, and the general idea of preprocessing for fast updates or queries.

05

String Algorithms

Prefix-function / KMP, Z-function, rolling hashes, tries, suffix structures, and pattern-matching techniques.

06

Advanced Dynamic Programming

State compression, digit DP, tree DP, interval DP, and recognizing hidden state transitions.

07

Randomized Algorithms

Randomized selection, hashing, probabilistic guarantees, and the distinction between Las Vegas and Monte Carlo algorithms.

08

Flows, Matching & Optimization

Maximum flow, bipartite matching, min-cut duality, and how combinatorial optimization extends the toolkit.

How to choose what comes next

For interviews

Prioritize trees, heaps, graph traversal, binary-search patterns, dynamic programming, tries, and common interval/range problems.

For competitive programming

Add DSU, shortest paths, MST, Fenwick/segment trees, string algorithms, advanced DP, number theory, and flows.

For computer science foundations

Spend more time on correctness proofs, recurrences, randomized algorithms, amortized analysis, and complexity theory.

For data science / ML

Keep the core toolkit, then emphasize numerical algorithms, linear algebra, optimization, randomized methods, and scalable data processing.
